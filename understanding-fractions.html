Understanding Fractions

A Complete Guide to Fractions for Primary Students

What is a Fraction?

A fraction is a way of representing a part of a whole number or quantity. When you divide something into equal parts, each part is called a fraction of the whole.

Example: If you have a pizza and cut it into 4 equal slices, each slice is 1/4 (one quarter) of the pizza.

Parts of a Fraction

Every fraction has two main parts:

  • Numerator: The number on top, showing how many parts you have
  • Denominator: The number at the bottom, showing how many equal parts the whole is divided into
In the fraction 3/5:
- 3 is the numerator (you have 3 parts)
- 5 is the denominator (the whole is divided into 5 parts)

Types of Fractions

1. Proper Fractions

The numerator is smaller than the denominator. The fraction is less than 1.

Examples: 1/2, 2/3, 3/4, 5/8

2. Improper Fractions

The numerator is larger than or equal to the denominator. The fraction is 1 or more.

Examples: 5/3, 7/4, 8/8

3. Mixed Numbers

A combination of a whole number and a proper fraction.

Examples: 2½, 3¾, 1⅖

Comparing Fractions

When Denominators are the Same

Compare the numerators. The fraction with the larger numerator is the larger fraction.

Example: 3/5 > 2/5 (because 3 > 2)

When Denominators are Different

Find a common denominator, then compare.

Example: Compare 1/2 and 2/3
Common denominator is 6
1/2 = 3/6 and 2/3 = 4/6
Therefore, 2/3 > 1/2

Adding and Subtracting Fractions

Same Denominator

Add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator the same.

Adding: 2/5 + 1/5 = 3/5
Subtracting: 4/7 - 2/7 = 2/7

Different Denominators

Find a common denominator first, then add or subtract.

Example: 1/2 + 1/4
Common denominator = 4
1/2 = 2/4, so 2/4 + 1/4 = 3/4

Multiplying and Dividing Fractions

Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together.

Example: 2/3 × 3/4 = (2 × 3)/(3 × 4) = 6/12 = 1/2

Dividing Fractions

Keep the first fraction, flip the second fraction, then multiply.

Example: 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = 3/4 × 2/1 = 6/4 = 3/2

Simplifying Fractions

A fraction is simplified when the numerator and denominator have no common factors except 1. Divide both by their greatest common factor.

Example: 6/9 can be simplified to 2/3 (divide both by 3)
💡 Tip: Always simplify your answer to its lowest terms!

Real-World Applications

  • Cooking (½ cup of flour, ¾ teaspoon of salt)
  • Time (half past = ½ past)
  • Money (25 cents = ¼ of a naira)
  • Sports (winning 3 out of 4 games = ¾ win rate)

Practice Questions

  1. What is 1/4 + 2/4?
  2. Simplify 8/12
  3. Compare: 3/5 or 2/3 (which is larger?)
  4. 2/3 × 3/4 = ?
  5. Convert 7/3 to a mixed number
  6. If a pizza has 8 slices and you eat 3, what fraction did you eat?
  7. 3/4 ÷ 1/2 = ?
  8. What is 1/2 + 1/3?
Key Points to Remember:
  • Numerator = number of parts you have
  • Denominator = total number of equal parts
  • Same denominator → add/subtract numerators only
  • Always simplify your final answer
  • Fractions are all around us in daily life!
